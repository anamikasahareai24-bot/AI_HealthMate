TOPIC: FEVER

Fever means a temporary rise in body temperature. It commonly happens when the body is responding to an infection or another illness.

Common symptoms may include feeling hot, chills, sweating, headache, body aches, weakness, and increased thirst.

Common causes include viral infections, bacterial infections, and some inflammatory conditions.

Persistent, very high, or concerning fever, especially when accompanied by difficulty breathing, confusion, severe headache, stiff neck, seizures, or severe weakness, needs prompt medical attention.

TOPIC: DIABETES

Diabetes is a condition in which blood glucose levels become too high because the body does not produce enough insulin or does not use insulin effectively.

Common symptoms can include increased thirst, frequent urination, tiredness, blurred vision, and unexplained weight changes.

Diabetes can be managed with appropriate medical care, lifestyle measures, monitoring, and treatment recommended by a healthcare professional.

A diagnosis of diabetes requires proper medical evaluation and blood glucose testing.


---

TOPIC: MIGRAINE

Migraine is a neurological condition that can cause recurring headaches.

A migraine may be associated with nausea, vomiting, and sensitivity to light or sound. Some people may experience warning symptoms called aura before or during an attack.

Migraine symptoms can vary between people.

Recurring or severe headaches should be evaluated by a healthcare professional, especially when the pattern changes or unusual symptoms occur.


---

TOPIC: DENGUE

Dengue is a viral infection transmitted mainly by infected mosquitoes.

Common symptoms can include fever, headache, pain behind the eyes, muscle and joint pain, nausea, vomiting, and rash.

Dengue can range from a mild illness to a serious condition.

Warning signs such as severe abdominal pain, repeated vomiting, bleeding, extreme weakness, restlessness, or difficulty breathing require urgent medical attention.

A healthcare professional should evaluate suspected dengue. Testing may be needed to confirm the infection.


---

TOPIC: ANEMIA

Anemia is a condition in which the blood does not have enough healthy red blood cells or hemoglobin to carry oxygen effectively.

Common symptoms may include tiredness, weakness, dizziness, headache, shortness of breath, or pale skin.

Iron deficiency is one common cause, but anemia can have several different causes.

The underlying cause should be identified by a healthcare professional before treatment is decided.


---

TOPIC: HEMOGLOBIN

Hemoglobin is a protein inside red blood cells that carries oxygen from the lungs to the rest of the body.

A low hemoglobin level can be associated with anemia and may occur due to causes such as nutritional deficiencies, blood loss, or other medical conditions.

A high hemoglobin level can occur for different reasons, including dehydration or conditions associated with increased red blood cell production.

The meaning of a hemoglobin result depends on the laboratory reference range, age, sex, health conditions, and other test results.


---

TOPIC: WHITE BLOOD CELLS

White blood cells, also called WBCs or leukocytes, are part of the immune system and help the body respond to infections and other conditions.

A high WBC count can occur with infections, inflammation, stress, or some medications and medical conditions.

A low WBC count can have several causes, including some infections, medications, nutritional problems, or medical conditions.

A WBC result should be interpreted using the laboratory reference range and the person's overall health information.


---

TOPIC: BLOOD PRESSURE

Blood pressure is the force of blood pushing against the walls of blood vessels.

It is usually recorded using two numbers: systolic pressure and diastolic pressure.

Persistently high blood pressure is called hypertension and can increase the risk of cardiovascular and kidney problems.

A single abnormal reading does not always establish a diagnosis. Blood pressure should be measured correctly and interpreted by a healthcare professional when readings remain abnormal.


---

TOPIC: HIGH CHOLESTEROL

Cholesterol is a waxy substance that the body needs for several important functions.

Different types of cholesterol are measured in a lipid profile, including LDL and HDL cholesterol.

High LDL cholesterol can contribute to buildup of fatty deposits in arteries and may increase cardiovascular risk.

Cholesterol results should be interpreted together with other health and risk factors rather than from one value alone.


---

TOPIC: COMMON COLD

The common cold is a common viral infection affecting the upper respiratory tract.

Symptoms may include runny or blocked nose, sneezing, sore throat, cough, and mild tiredness.

Most colds improve with time.

Severe breathing difficulty, persistent or worsening symptoms, or other concerning symptoms should be evaluated by a healthcare professional.


---

TOPIC: COUGH

A cough is a reflex that helps clear the airways of mucus, irritants, or foreign material.

Cough can occur with infections such as the common cold, allergies, asthma, or other respiratory conditions.

The cause of a cough cannot always be determined from the cough alone.

A persistent, severe, or worsening cough, especially with breathing difficulty, chest pain, or coughing up blood, needs medical evaluation.


---

TOPIC: ASTHMA

Asthma is a condition in which the airways can become inflamed and narrowed, causing breathing symptoms.

Common symptoms include wheezing, coughing, chest tightness, and shortness of breath.

Symptoms can vary over time and may be triggered by factors such as allergens, exercise, infections, smoke, or air pollution.

Severe difficulty breathing or rapidly worsening breathing symptoms require urgent medical attention.


---

TOPIC: THYROID

The thyroid is a gland in the neck that produces hormones involved in metabolism and many body functions.

An underactive thyroid is called hypothyroidism. It may be associated with tiredness, feeling unusually cold, constipation, and other symptoms.

An overactive thyroid is called hyperthyroidism. It may be associated with fast heartbeat, increased sweating, heat intolerance, and other symptoms.

Thyroid conditions are evaluated using symptoms, medical history, and blood tests such as thyroid hormone tests.


---

TOPIC: DEHYDRATION

Dehydration occurs when the body loses more fluid than it takes in.

Possible symptoms include thirst, dry mouth, tiredness, dizziness, headache, and reduced urination.

Dehydration can happen because of vomiting, diarrhea, fever, heavy sweating, or not drinking enough fluids.

Severe dehydration can be serious and requires prompt medical attention.


---

TOPIC: HEADACHE

A headache is pain or discomfort in the head or surrounding areas.

Headaches can have many causes, including tension, migraine, dehydration, illness, lack of sleep, or other conditions.

The location, duration, severity, and associated symptoms can help a healthcare professional understand the possible cause.

A sudden severe headache, or a headache associated with confusion, fainting, weakness, vision problems, seizures, or other serious symptoms requires urgent medical evaluation.


---

TOPIC: NAUSEA

Nausea is the feeling that you may vomit.

It can occur with infections, migraine, motion sickness, digestive problems, medications, or many other conditions.

Nausea by itself does not identify a specific disease.

Persistent vomiting, inability to keep fluids down, severe abdominal pain, confusion, or signs of dehydration require medical evaluation.


---

TOPIC: BODY TEMPERATURE

Body temperature can vary depending on the person, time of day, activity, environment, and method of measurement.

An elevated temperature can occur during infections and other illnesses.

Temperature should be interpreted together with symptoms and the measurement method.

If an elevated temperature is persistent or accompanied by serious symptoms, medical advice should be sought.


---

TOPIC: CBC

A complete blood count, commonly called CBC, is a blood test that measures several blood components.

Common CBC parameters include hemoglobin, red blood cells, white blood cells, hematocrit, and platelets.

CBC results can provide useful information about blood cells, but an abnormal result does not automatically identify a specific disease.

Results should be interpreted using the laboratory's reference ranges and the person's clinical context.


---

TOPIC: PLATELETS

Platelets are blood cells that help the body form clots and stop bleeding.

A low platelet count is called thrombocytopenia and can have many causes.

A high platelet count can also occur for different reasons, including inflammation and certain medical conditions.

Platelet results should be interpreted using the laboratory reference range and other clinical information.


---

TOPIC: MEDICAL REPORT

A medical report contains laboratory or clinical information collected during a medical evaluation.

Reference ranges may differ between laboratories and can depend on factors such as age, sex, and testing methods.

An abnormal laboratory value does not necessarily mean that a person has a disease.

Medical reports should be interpreted together with symptoms, medical history, and professional medical advice.


---

TOPIC: WHEN TO SEEK URGENT MEDICAL HELP

Some symptoms may indicate a medical emergency.

Urgent medical evaluation is appropriate for symptoms such as severe difficulty breathing, severe chest pain, loss of consciousness, new severe confusion, seizures, sudden weakness, or other rapidly worsening serious symptoms.

A health information chatbot cannot determine whether an emergency is occurring.

When symptoms appear severe or life-threatening, contact local emergency medical services or seek immediate professional medical care.
